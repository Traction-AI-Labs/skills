# Patterns that give model prose away

Use with the tests in SKILL.md. The fix column never adds anything; when a fix would need a fact the draft does not have, cut instead. Examples here are invented for illustration.

## Act on sight

These are rarely a writer's own choice.

| Pattern | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Contrast frame | "It's not about the tools, it's about the people." "Less paperwork, more patients." | State the second half plainly. Keep a contrast only when the reader really holds the first view |
| Closing moral | A last line that turns the piece into a saying: "In the end, good service is just good listening." | Cut it. End on the last real point |
| Self-answered question | "The result? Bookings doubled." "Why does this matter? Because..." | State the answer as a sentence |
| Announcing the text | "I'm writing to let you know...", "In this guide we'll cover...", "Here's the thing:" | Start with what follows the announcement |
| Stock opener or closer | "In today's fast-moving world", "I hope this finds you well", "Feel free to reach out", "Exciting times ahead" | Cut |
| Engagement bait | A closing "What's your take? Let me know below 👇" on a post | Cut, unless the writer is genuinely asking something specific |
| Staged feeling | A short emotional beat dropped in on cue: "That stung.", "Honestly? I was floored." | Cut the beat, or fold the feeling into the sentence that carries the fact. Never add one |
| Pre-emptive empathy | "I know this may be frustrating", "Rest assured", "We truly appreciate your patience and understanding" | Cut. Keep a real offer, apology or thanks the draft makes, said once and plainly |

## Act when they cluster

One alone may be the writer's. Three in a paragraph is a template.

| Pattern | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Payoff clause | A fact followed by its benefit: "..., so you can focus on what matters", "..., which means less time on admin", "..., helping teams move faster" | Keep the fact. Cut the tail unless it states a separate claim the draft makes; then give that claim its own plain sentence |
| Slogan pair | "Plan faster. Ship sooner." Two parallel imperatives or fragments | Cut, or merge into the sentence that carries the fact |
| Section tagline | A one-line slogan paragraph opening each section of a page | Cut; let the section start with its first fact |
| Negative listing | "No forms. No waiting. No fuss. Just results." | Say what the thing does. Keep any item that is a real claim ("no lock-in contract") |
| Groups of three | Three adjectives or clauses where the content supports two, or the third is a synonym | Drop the padding item only; never drop a real one |
| Virtue adjectives | "fast, friendly service", "smart, simple and secure" | Say what makes it so if the draft says; otherwise use the plain noun |
| Bolded run-in phrases | Each paragraph or list item opening with a bold aphorism | In posts and emails fold the point into the sentence. In documents, headings and labels stay |
| Inflated significance | "a pivotal moment", "a testament to", "plays a key role in" | State the fact the phrase is inflating, or cut |
| Vague authority | "Experts agree", "studies show", "many businesses find" | Cut unless the draft names the source |
| Stacked qualifiers | "may potentially", "could possibly help" | Keep one qualifier. Keep every qualifier that marks a real exception or a legal limit |
| Bookends | The opening and the closing restate the same frame | Keep one |

## Whole-piece templates

Models fill the stock skeleton of the genre in the order the brief gave them the facts.

- **Personal post:** hook line with an emoji, one line of context, "Here's what I learned:", three bolded lessons, a round of thanks, a closing question, a block of generic hashtags. Keep the content; drop the frame parts that carry none; keep a specific hashtag (an event or company name) and cut generic ones.
- **About page:** "Our story / Our mission / What we do / Our approach" headings on a short page. Keep the content in plain paragraphs; keep a heading only where the page is long enough to need it.
- **Short email:** headings such as "Summary / Next steps" over a few lines. Use sentences, with a list only for two or more things the reader must do.
- **Announcement:** "We're thrilled to announce..." then features, then "Stay tuned". Start with what changed and for whom.

## Words and punctuation

Word choice is the weakest signal, and the list goes out of date with every model generation. Replace these only when they are padding, with the plain word the sentence needs:

delve, tapestry, landscape (figurative), leverage (as a verb), seamless, robust, elevate, empower, foster, unlock, streamline, navigate (figurative), multifaceted, pivotal, game-changer, testament.

Dashes: some models still lean on the em dash. Replace it with what the sentence needs (a comma, a colon, brackets or a full stop), and vary the choice. A text where every dash became a full stop reads as edited by a model too.

## Edits that read as AI themselves

- Chopping sentences into short fragments for "punch".
- Adding a personal aside, a reaction or an opinion to make the text "warmer".
- Swapping words for synonyms with no reason.
- Making every sentence the same length.
- Turning a list or a table into prose, or prose into a list, without being asked.
